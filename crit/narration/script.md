# CRIT — narration script

Voice: direct, discerning, creatively literate. Automated checks should sound rigorous but deliberately incomplete.

## 01 — Brief lock
Campaign review begins with a structured brief bound to version six. Any amendment creates a new version; the standard cannot be rewritten after the work arrives.

## 02 — Work intake
The film becomes fourteen addressable components. OpenRouter routes vision, strategy, voice, comprehension, and summary tasks separately so one model does not review its own interpretation of the work.

## 03 — Strategy
A reasoning model compares the concept to the locked audience and proposition and cites the brief line behind its verdict. The work is strategically on brief.

## 04 — Substantiation
Rules find explicit claims while a multimodal model surfaces implied claims created by image, edit, and voice-over together. Live proof clears three statements and rejects the implication that the product eliminates review.

## 05 — Voice
The legally supportable voice-over still falls below the calibrated restraint floor. Only that component regenerates, once, and must pass the original rubric before the review continues.

## 06 — Craft and access
Contrast, delivered type size, captions, safe areas, and timing are measured in code. A different vision family watches the film small and muted and reports that the proposition survives but the differentiation does not.

## 07 — Rights
Detection surfaces a licensed music cue and a synthetic-content disclosure. A named rights owner—not a model—confirms the license, territory, term, and placement before the rights gate clears delivery.

## 08 — Direction
Every automated check passes. The Creative Director still returns revise: correct, polished, and inert. The immutable record preserves that reason and sends the missing human dimension back to the evaluation bench.
