# Overview

AuScan is an AI-assisted geospatial research proof of concept, not a production mining system. It explores whether vision models and multisensor satellite data can help identify terrain signatures associated with gold prospectivity.

## Adam's Role

Adam defined the research workflow, assembled sources, built and tested the interface with AI assistance, wrote the documentation, and designed human validation and export steps. He is not claiming to be a geologist or remote-sensing scientist.

## Architecture and Data

React artifact; Claude and Claude Vision; ESRI, NASA EMIT, Landsat, ASTER, Sentinel-2, and USGS-related research; pattern learning, comparison, search-grid workflow, persistence, and Markdown export. External validation references USGS MRDS.

## Outcomes and Limits

The prototype demonstrates multimodal research, evidence assembly, geospatial UX, and disciplined research boundaries. A 72 percent public-surface validation figure is explicitly excluded from canonical public claims until Adam confirms and documents its methodology.

## Spoken Explanation

AuScan is a research instrument Adam built to test whether Claude Vision and different satellite sensors could identify terrain patterns worth human investigation. It is evidence of multimodal prototyping, not a claim that AI can reliably discover mines.
