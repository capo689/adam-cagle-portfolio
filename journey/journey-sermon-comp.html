MEEK.
SURRENDER WINS.
One message. More ways to take it with you.Open on YouTube ↗

Strength, surrendered.

What if the blessed life begins with letting go of control? This study explores meekness as strength surrendered to God: trusting his direction without losing the courage to act.

Through the picture of a trained horse and the example of Jesus, the guide invites us to rethink power, practice humility, and consider where we are still insisting on our own way.

Three ideas to carry with you

01
Meekness begins with surrender.

Recognizing our need for God leads to trusting him with our decisions.

02
Meekness is not weakness.

Surrender directs our strength. It does not erase it.

03
God promises the meek an inheritance.

We can entrust the outcome to God instead of forcing our own way.

Bring the message into your week.

Use these questions on your own, with a friend, or around a table with your group.

01   Meekness begins with surrender

Meekness moves us from recognizing our need for God to surrendering our choices and direction to him.

Read: Matthew 5:3–5 · Romans 12:1 · Proverbs 3:5–6

  1. What is the difference between recognizing your need for God and surrendering to him?
  2. Which area of your life is hardest to entrust to God right now?
02   Meekness is not weakness

A trained horse keeps its strength but learns to respond to direction. In the supplied guide, this illustrates strength yielded to God rather than strength lost.

Read: Matthew 11:28–30 · Matthew 26:52–53 · John 13

  1. Where do you confuse gentleness with weakness?
  2. How does Jesus’ example change the way you think about power?
03   Trust God with the outcome

The guide connects meekness with trusting God’s timing and promises rather than taking control of every outcome.

Read: Psalm 37:5–6, 11 · 1 Peter 5:6

  1. What outcome are you trying to control?
  2. What could it look like to act faithfully while releasing that outcome?
