Journey Church · Live-site review · October 24, 2026

Accessibility & privacy:
what the public site shows

Confirmed accessibility barriers and privacy-disclosure gaps. Neither ADA liability nor complete GDPR compliance can be established from the public website alone.

The conclusion

The sampled website is not ready for a WCAG 2.2 AA conformance claim. The review reproduced keyboard-navigation barriers, unnamed sermon links, insufficient footer-link contrast, and missing page-language declarations. These are practical access problems whether or not a particular law requires remediation.

GDPR compliance is unverified. Journey-specific privacy information was not found on the inspected pages or form collection points. Sensitive collection and third-party embeds require further review. This is not a finding that Journey has unlawfully disclosed information or set a particular tracking cookie.

Legal scope matters

ADA: The Department of Justice states that religious organizations are exempt from Title III. Journey's religious-entity status and the operator of any separate activity matter. This report does not apply the government-only Title II web rule to Journey. Other applicable duties need their own assessment. DOJ Title III guidance · DOJ technical assistance manual.

Accessibility target: Use WCAG 2.2 AA as the technical acceptance target for the new site. An automated score or a purchased accessibility widget would not establish whole-site conformance.

GDPR: Applicability can arise through an EU establishment or relevant offering of services to, or monitoring of, people in the EU. Simply being viewable from Europe does not settle the question. Journey's actual audience targeting and processing must be assessed. European Commission applicability guidance · EDPB territorial-scope guidance.

Cookie/device-access requirements also involve national implementations of EU ePrivacy rules; they are not interchangeable with GDPR. CNIL guidance on third-party videos explains why the provider's actual technical behavior must be checked.

What was checked

Nine main-site pages: /, /visit, /journey-kids, /contact-us, /prayer-request, /care, /giving, /sermons, /media.

Six fully loaded public Church Center forms: Prayer / Praise Request, Urgent Prayer Requests, Hospital visit, eScoop signup, Connection card, Child dedication.

Methods: live browser inspection; DOM and accessibility-tree inspection; keyboard interaction; a measured footer-color comparison with visual background confirmation; homepage checks at 1440px, 706px and 320px widths; inspection of the featured YouTube player's settings; public privacy and form-copy review; official regulatory guidance.

Read-only review: no form submissions, registrations, donations, signups or account changes. No axe/Lighthouse scan or full assistive-technology test matrix was run. This is a targeted public-site review, not a complete WCAG evaluation, legal opinion or certification. The browser session was not established as a clean EU session; cookies, storage writes and network payloads were not fully captured.

Evidence, direct links and fixes

A1 · Accessibility · High

Navigation is not reliably keyboard accessible

Reproduced

Observed: At a 1440px viewport, tabbing from the desktop About control moved to Sermons while its submenu remained display:none. Enter and Arrow Down did not expose its links. At 706px, the visible hamburger was a div with no role, accessible name or tabindex; Tab traversed off-canvas navigation while the menu remained closed.

See it: https://journeyinbend.com/
On desktop, use Tab to reach About, then try Enter, Arrow Down and Tab. At a narrow width, try to reach and operate the hamburger without a pointer. A developer can inspect #sp-header #nav_about and #sp-nav-button.

Relevant standard / legal question: WCAG 2.1.1 Keyboard; 4.1.2 Name, Role, Value; focus visibility needs remediation and retesting.

Action: Use real disclosure buttons with accessible names and expanded state. Support Enter/Space, sensible focus order and Escape. Remove closed off-canvas menus from keyboard and accessibility navigation.

Owner: Website developer / platform support

A2 · Accessibility · High

Nine sermon thumbnail links have no descriptive accessible name

Confirmed markup and accessibility-tree issue

Observed: All nine image links under Previous Sermon Series had empty alt text, no link text, no aria-label, no aria-labelledby and no title. The accessibility tree exposed raw YouTube destinations instead of meaningful series names.

See it: https://journeyinbend.com/sermons
Open Previous Sermon Series. Use a screen reader's links list, or inspect each thumbnail anchor and its image. The image-only anchors have alt="" and no replacement name.

Relevant standard / legal question: WCAG 1.1.1 Non-text Content; 2.4.4 Link Purpose; 4.1.2 Name, Role, Value.

Action: Give every linked thumbnail a meaningful series or message title, preferably as visible linked text with an appropriate image alternative.

Owner: Content editor + website developer

A3 · Accessibility · Medium

Footer text links have insufficient contrast

Measured and visually checked

Observed: Contact Us, Journey in Redmond, Give Online and Watch Live use rgb(127,140,141), or #7f8c8d, against a white background. Calculated contrast is approximately 3.48:1. Text was 15px at 320px viewport and 16.5px in the desktop check, below the large-text threshold.

See it: https://journeyinbend.com/
Scroll to the footer. Measure #7f8c8d against #ffffff with a contrast checker; inspect the computed link color and size. The white background was checked visually, not inferred solely from ancestor styles.

Relevant standard / legal question: WCAG 1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum): 4.5:1 for this text.

Action: Darken the footer link color to a measured ratio of at least 4.5:1; also check hover and focus states.

Owner: Website designer / developer

A4 · Accessibility · Medium

Page language is not declared

Confirmed on all nine sampled main-site pages

Observed: Neither lang nor xml:lang was present on the document element on Home, Visit, Kids, Contact, Prayer, Care, Giving, Sermons or Media.

See it: https://journeyinbend.com/
Inspect the opening html element on any sampled page. document.documentElement.getAttribute('lang') returned null.

Relevant standard / legal question: WCAG 3.1.1 Language of Page.

Action: Set the site's document language to English, such as lang="en", and identify passages in another language where needed.

Owner: Website developer / platform support

A5 · Accessibility · Medium

Logo and social links lack meaningful accessible names

Confirmed markup and accessibility-tree issue

Observed: The logo anchor contains an image without alt text and no replacement name. Footer social anchors contain only icon-font elements, without accessible labels. The accessibility tree exposed a URL for the logo and icon glyphs for social destinations.

See it: https://journeyinbend.com/
Inspect #sp-logo a and footer .sp-social-holder a, or read their names with assistive technology.

Relevant standard / legal question: WCAG 1.1.1; 2.4.4; 4.1.2.

Action: Name the logo link Journey Church — Home and label each social destination. Hide decorative icon glyphs from assistive technology.

Owner: Website developer / platform support

A6 · Accessibility · Medium

Video and map frames lack descriptive titles

Confirmed markup issue

Observed: The YouTube frames on Home and Sermons, Google Maps frame on Visit, and Subsplash frame on Media have no title attribute. These are content frames; the finding excludes the many blank layout/resizer frames inserted by the platform.

See it: https://journeyinbend.com/visit
Inspect iframe[src] on Visit, Home, Sermons and Media. The actual content frames have source URLs but no descriptive title.

Relevant standard / legal question: WCAG frame-identification remediation; relevant to 2.4.1 Bypass Blocks and 4.1.2 Name, Role, Value. Some embedded documents expose their own titles, which does not excuse omitting a clear frame label.

Action: Add concise titles such as Map to Journey Church in Bend and the actual sermon title. Check the resulting screen-reader frame list.

Owner: Website developer / platform support

A7 · Accessibility · Medium

Media-library search relies on a placeholder

Confirmed missing explicit labels; assistive-technology retest needed

Observed: The library search input has placeholder="search..." with no label, aria-label or aria-labelledby. The category select also lacks an explicit label in the inspected markup. The search field appeared unnamed in the observed accessibility tree. Placeholder fallback differs between browser/assistive-technology combinations.

See it: https://journeyinbend.com/media
Inspect the search input and category select in Media Library. Check the field's announcement on desktop and narrow layouts.

Relevant standard / legal question: WCAG 3.3.2 Labels or Instructions; verify 4.1.2 in the target browser/screen-reader combinations.

Action: Add visible, programmatically associated labels for Search messages and Browse by. Do not rely on placeholder text alone.

Owner: Website developer / platform support

P1 · Privacy · High

Journey-specific privacy information was not found at the inspected collection points

Public disclosure gap observed; legal applicability unresolved

Observed: No privacy/cookie notice links were found on the nine sampled main-site pages. The six inspected Church Center forms link to Planning Center's Privacy Policy; no Journey-specific notice covering its own handling was visible. The regular and urgent prayer forms do disclose sharing with prayer, staff and/or pastoral teams.

See it: https://jnc.churchcenter.com/people/forms/104741
Check the main-site footer, then the prayer form and its Privacy Policy destination. Look for Journey's purposes, retention, rights process and contact for privacy requests.

Relevant standard / legal question: If GDPR applies, assess Articles 12–14 transparency duties; a vendor policy alone does not establish that Journey's controller disclosures are complete.

Action: Publish a Journey-specific notice and link it where information is collected. State purposes, legal bases, recipients, retention criteria, rights and applicable transfers. Have Journey verify the actual practices before publication.

Owner: Journey leadership / privacy owner with legal review

P2 · Privacy · High

Hospital-visit form collects health and religious information

Sensitive collection confirmed; lawful basis and controls not observable

Observed: The form asks about hospitalization, allergies and whether the person follows Jesus, and collects a reason for hospitalization. Its wording permits information about another person. No Journey-specific sensitive-data notice or explanation of authority to provide another person's information was visible.

See it: https://jnc.churchcenter.com/people/forms/723924
Open Request a hospital visit and read the questions without entering any data. Check the introductory text and footer notice.

Relevant standard / legal question: If GDPR applies, evaluate Articles 5, 6, 9 and potentially 14. Religious nonprofit conditions in Article 9(2)(d) are limited, not a blanket exemption.

Action: Reduce collection to what is necessary for the visit; explain whose data is requested and why; establish authority and the applicable legal basis/Article 9 condition; restrict recipients and retention. A consent checkbox alone is not a complete solution.

Owner: Journey care lead + privacy/legal owner

P3 · Privacy · High review priority

Third-party embeds load without an observed consent choice

Embeds and absent visible choice observed; tracking behavior unverified

Observed: YouTube content loaded on Home/Sermons, Google Maps was directly embedded on Visit, and Subsplash loaded on Media. No consent banner or cookie-settings control was observed in this browser session. Exact cookies, storage writes, regional behavior and any prior browser consent were not established.

See it: https://journeyinbend.com/
Open Home, Visit and Media in a clean browser session. Use network/storage inspection from the relevant region before interaction, after rejection and after acceptance. This deeper trace remains required.

Relevant standard / legal question: Assess applicable EU ePrivacy rules and GDPR where personal data is processed. An embed or an absent banner alone does not prove unlawful tracking.

Action: Inventory actual storage and requests. Where consent is required, prevent nonessential loading until a valid choice, provide an equally easy refusal, and allow withdrawal. Offer a privacy-preserving video/map placeholder or external link as appropriate.

Owner: Website developer + privacy owner

P4 · Privacy · Medium

Newsletter signup needs clearer standalone information

Collection and explanatory gaps observed

Observed: The eScoop form shows its title, name/email fields, optional phone fields and a Submit button, without visible explanatory copy about email frequency, withdrawal or Journey's use of the information. The homepage describes the newsletter as weekly. No email was submitted, so unsubscribe handling was not tested.

See it: https://jnc.churchcenter.com/people/forms/118361
Open the signup form directly and assess what a person learns without visiting the homepage first.

Relevant standard / legal question: Transparency and purpose clarity; assess consent and applicable electronic-marketing rules. A separate checkbox is not automatically required for a dedicated signup form.

Action: Explain the newsletter and frequency, label the action clearly, link Journey's notice and explain unsubscribe. Remove optional phone collection unless there is a documented need; test unsubscribe and preference handling separately.

Owner: Journey communications + privacy owner

P5 · Privacy · Medium

Connection card requires phone and address; child form collects family information

Data-minimization review item

Observed: The connection card marks phone and address as required. The child-dedication form collects a child's name and age plus who will join on stage. Necessity, permissions, retention and access restrictions cannot be established from the public forms.

See it: https://jnc.churchcenter.com/people/forms/841370
Inspect the required fields on the connection card and the child-dedication form at /people/forms/396853. Do not submit test personal information.

Relevant standard / legal question: If GDPR applies, assess data minimization, lawful basis, child-data handling and retention. Collecting a child's data is not by itself proof that the GDPR's Article 8 online-consent rule applies.

Action: Document why each field is needed, make unnecessary fields optional or remove them, and explain child/parent data use. Confirm any separate photo/video permissions rather than assuming form submission grants them.

Owner: Journey communications / kids lead + privacy owner

What is working, and what is not being alleged

  • The sampled Church Center forms declare English, provide a Skip to content link, and expose labels for the inspected visible form controls. That is a positive observation, not certification of the complete submission flow.
  • The regular and urgent prayer forms disclose sharing with ministry teams. The urgent form also provides response expectations. The concern is incomplete visible privacy information, not a claim that all sharing is undisclosed.
  • The homepage document width remained 320px at a 320px viewport. No page-level horizontal overflow was measured in that check; text zoom and every component still need testing.
  • The featured YouTube player exposed a Captions setting. Caption availability across the archive, accuracy, synchronization and live captions were not validated. The report does not claim all sermons lack captions.
  • Empty image alternative text can be correct for decoration. The firm finding concerns image-only links with no meaningful name; not every empty alt attribute is an error.
  • Multiple H1 headings and the absence of a skip link were observed, but neither is counted here as an automatic failure without considering the full structure and available bypass mechanisms.
  • Early contrast candidates on photo/overlay sections were excluded because ancestor background colors did not represent the visible background. Only the verified white-footer measurement is reported as a contrast failure.
  • A consent checkbox is not automatically required for every form. The legal basis, purpose, user action, notice and evidence of consent where used must be considered together.

What remains to establish GDPR compliance

The public review cannot verify these internal facts. Treat them as evidence requests, not discovered violations:

  • Whether Journey targets or monitors people in the EU, and which processing falls within GDPR.
  • The purposes and legal bases for each form; any Article 9 condition for health or religious information; how third-party submissions are handled.
  • Retention and deletion rules, permissions, staff access, and incident procedures.
  • Contracts and roles for Planning Center, Subsplash/SnapPages, Google/YouTube, Monday forms, Brushfire, Overflow and any email provider; international-transfer arrangements where required. Presence of a link alone does not establish a controller/processor relationship.
  • How people exercise access, correction, deletion, objection and consent withdrawal rights where applicable; how requests are completed in downstream services.
  • A clean-session, region-appropriate network/storage test before interaction, after refusal, after acceptance and after withdrawal. This must identify actual cookies and payloads, not merely list third-party domains.
  • Actual email unsubscribe behavior, records of subscription choices, and any separate SMS permission practices.
  • Children's data handling and photo/video permissions. No assumption that a dedication signup permits publishing a child's image.

Relevant legal text: GDPR, especially Articles 5, 6, 9, 12–14, 28, 32 and Chapter V. Article 9's religious-body condition has limits; it is not a general exemption from GDPR. For consent-dependent trackers, consult CNIL's consent and refusal guidance.

Recommended order of work

  1. Repair access to core content: navigation, sermon links, language, meaningful link/frame labels and footer contrast. Most of these are shared-template changes.
  2. Review sensitive forms: start with hospital visits and prayer requests. Assign a Journey owner, verify necessary fields and actual handling, then publish accurate collection notices.
  3. Publish and connect Journey's privacy information: make it accessible from the site footer and every collection point; keep provider notices as additional information.
  4. Verify consent behavior: test real requests and device storage in clean sessions and relevant regions, then configure blocking and choices where needed.
  5. Complete accessibility coverage: screen-reader testing, 200% text resize, reflow, mobile-menu focus, form errors and recovery, captions, care-resource PDFs, and registration/giving flows with appropriate test facilities.

For the new site, make these acceptance criteria part of the build. For planned AI search, add keyboard-accessible conversation controls, understandable loading/error states, accessible citations, and a specific privacy decision for search-query logs. Private care and prayer submissions must remain outside public RAG retrieval.
