# Working Style

## Ambiguous Problems

Adam typically begins by identifying the real outcome rather than accepting the first framing. His agency and product record shows a recurring sequence: discovery, problem definition, alternatives, selection, build, review, launch, and iteration. He is comfortable creating structure where no mature process exists.

## Pace and Iteration

Adam favors fast, substantive iterations, but expects each round to be internally checked. He does not object to revision; he objects to preventable revision caused by failing to reread the work, ignoring prior feedback, or introducing a new defect while fixing an old one.

## Standards

His feedback shows a strong preference for finished, usable outputs over partial fragments. For résumé and application work, he expects a complete cut-and-paste block, checked for repetition, unsupported claims, and downstream conflicts before delivery. More generally, this reflects a preference for collaborators who own quality rather than transferring verification back to him.

## Communication

Adam is direct, informal, and comfortable using humor or profanity under frustration. The underlying signal is usually specific: the work drifted, repeated an error, ignored context, or created unnecessary labor. A collaborator should respond to the concrete failure rather than tone-police him or offer vague reassurance.

## Decision Making

Adam combines instinct with evidence. He values measurable outcomes, competitor analysis, testing, and real usage, but he also treats craft judgment as real expertise. He makes decisive calls when a role, claim, or product does not feel aligned and expects the evidence to be reconsidered rather than defended automatically.

## Use of AI Collaborators

Adam uses Claude and Codex extensively and has said they should receive equal mention in his development workflow. He uses them for research, architecture, writing, coding, testing, troubleshooting, documentation, and critique. He does not frame AI as the sole author: he supplies goals and domain judgment, chooses approaches, checks work, integrates components, and owns the outcome.

## What Energizes Him

- Building a useful system from an underdefined problem.
- Strong language, sharp concepts, and work with an actual point of view.
- Connecting creative judgment with technical execution.
- Seeing measurable business results.
- Teams that are capable, candid, low-ego, and close to making the work.
- AI products, agents, workflows, answer-engine visibility, and new interaction models.

## What Frustrates Him

- Repetition that a basic reread should have caught.
- Partial output when he requested a complete artifact.
- Generic prose that parrots a brief instead of writing.
- Unsupported claims that could damage credibility.
- Context loss that forces him to reteach an established process.
- Lazy acceptance of poor work or suggestions to submit something half-finished.

## Preferred Environment

Adam appears best suited to high-ownership teams where he can remain close to the work, cross boundaries between disciplines, and improve systems as well as individual deliverables. He is open to senior individual-contributor roles and does not require direct reports.

## Signals of Project Health

Going well: the problem is explicit, responsibilities are clear, artifacts improve across iterations, results are measured, and the team reuses what it learns. Going badly: requirements drift, the same defects recur, ownership becomes ambiguous, outputs cannot be trusted without line-by-line rework, or automation hides its failure modes.
