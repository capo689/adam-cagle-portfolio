01 — Operating Philosophy

Lead with one fundable wedge.

A strong organization's full vision is large. Applications never lead with the entire universe. They lead with the single program module that best matches the target funder's priorities, confirmed with the writer before any drafting begins.

Funders fund credible work that matches their priorities, serves a defined community, has a realistic plan, uses money responsibly, and can explain what will change.
Not this

The whole vision at once: every program, every site, every future phase crammed into a single ask. Breadth reads as risk, and risk does not get funded.

This

One community, one need, one funder-aligned program, one realistic budget, one honest impact plan. The clearest promise wins, not the biggest story.

02 — Non-Negotiables

Seven rules that protect the relationship.

Win rate and funder trust are assets. These rules exist to protect both. Break one and a single application can cost a relationship that took years to build.

01
Do not fabricate
Never invent outcomes, participant counts, testimonials, partner commitments, legal status, or eligibility. If a fact is missing, ask for it or mark it [NEEDS HUMAN INPUT].
02
Do not submit
The system builds drafts, checklists, and packages. It never submits, certifies, signs, or emails a funder without explicit human instruction.
03
Disclose AI honestly
If asked, disclose that AI assisted drafting, organization, formatting, and QA under human review. Never imply AI made program decisions or verified claims.
04
Respect exclusions
Flag and remove activities the funder excludes: deficit funding, endowments, lobbying, regranting, capital, and others. The funder profile governs.
05
Protect vulnerable people
No personal details about minors or vulnerable participants without confirmed consent, dignity, and privacy safeguards. Composite stories only when allowed and labeled.
06
Work from current requirements
Guidelines change every cycle. Never draft from memory. Fetch the live page or work from pasted current guidelines, labeled as user-confirmed.
07
Sanity-check the deadline
Passed: stop and recommend the next cycle. Under ten days: flag the slowest items to obtain and confirm intent. Pre-application step required: confirm it happened first.
03 — The Build

From target to package.

Confirm before you research. Research before you draft. Draft before you assemble. The order protects against the most expensive mistake: a beautiful application to the wrong funder.

1
Confirm
Interview & confirm the target

Which grant, which applicant, what legal status, direct or fiscal sponsor, what amount, what program, what geography, what deadline. No drafting on a weak or ineligible target.

2
Research
Build the funder profile

From current guidelines: best fit, posture, eligible applicants and request types, range, deadline, format, review criteria, exclusions, required attachments, and writing priorities.

3
Research
Run the fit triage

Score eligibility, exclusions, priority match, evidence, and budget readiness. If the fit is poor, say so and recommend a stronger module or a different funder.

4
Draft
Draft in the funder's exact format

Question order, character limits, and rubric targets from the profile. Every claim carries an evidence label. Mirror the funder's language without parroting it.

5
Draft
Build the budget & narrative

Totals computed programmatically, never eyeballed. Income and expenses reconcile. Secured, pending, planned, and in-kind support each identified. No ineligible costs.

6
Deliver
Assemble & render the package

Requirements matrix, fit memo, application draft, budget, attachments checklist, source appendix, and review gate. Rendered and visually inspected before delivery.

7
Deliver
Human review, then log & learn

A human approves each area and holds final submission authority. After the cycle, log the metrics and capture reusable, consent-cleared language for next time.

05 — Evidence Standard

Every claim wears a label.

Every statistic, outcome, budget number, participant count, date, and testimonial carries one of four labels. A source appendix maps each claim to its origin, confidence, and human reviewer.

Highest confidence
Verified fact
Primary source, official document, budget, program record, or user-provided evidence on file.
Attributed
User-provided
Stated by the organization, accepted in good faith, not yet independently verified.
Labeled
Assumption
Reasonable but unverified. Always marked as an assumption so a reviewer can confirm or strike it.
Open
Placeholder
Missing. Marked [NEEDS HUMAN INPUT] and surfaced in the Missing Inputs table, never buried.
06 — Drafting Voice

Clarity, warmth, confidence.

Human, funder-facing, specific, restrained. No hype, no vague inspiration, no exaggerated transformation claims. Most answers follow one disciplined arc.

Direct answer→ Community need→ Specific response→ Community voice→ Activities & timeline→ Outcomes→ Stewardship

Lead with

  • A direct answer to the question actually asked
  • Specific, timed activities over adjectives
  • Representation that goes beyond a survey where possible
  • Measurable impact and a realistic sustainability plan
  • The funder's own language, mirrored accurately

Strike on sight

  • Hype, inflated transformation, vague inspiration
  • Unsupported causation and round-number outcomes
  • Em dashes and double hyphens; restructure instead
  • Claims a population is prioritized when it is not true
  • Promises of autonomous tools running the program

11 — Log & Learn

The method compounds.

Every application leaves the organization stronger for the next one. The skill grows more valuable each cycle, not less.

01
Log the metrics

Amount requested, hours spent, edits required, outcome, and dollars won. Win rate becomes a number you can manage, not a feeling.

02
Capture the language

Approved descriptions, verified facts with sources and dates, and consent-cleared stories build an answer library that speeds every future draft.

03
Learn from the no

Request reviewer feedback, map each reason to a fix, set the next cycle as a target, and open the resubmission by noting what changed. Funders reward visible learning.

The best application is not the biggest story. It is the clearest promise: one community, one need, one funder-aligned program, and one human-reviewed application that makes the funder trust the work.
