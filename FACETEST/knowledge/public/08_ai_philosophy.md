## Working Hypotheses

- Frontier AI can improve SEO and answer-engine visibility when it directly analyzes site, search, competitor, and structured-data evidence rather than merely producing generic recommendations.
- Brand voice can scale without flattening if the system retains source context, generates alternatives, checks standards, and requires human editorial approval.
- Internal tools can outperform generic software when they encode the organization's actual workflow and remain maintainable.
- Recursive improvement is valuable only when evidence, approvals, and rollback prevent the system from learning the wrong lesson unchecked.

## Open Questions

- How much autonomy should agents receive as identity, payment, and assurance mechanisms mature?
- What forms of memory improve continuity without creating privacy or stale-context problems?
- How should evaluation balance factual reliability, usefulness, voice, speed, and cost?
- When does building a custom tool create leverage, and when does it create avoidable maintenance?
- How will conversational and agentic interfaces change conventional websites and software navigation?

## Views That Have Changed

No reliably documented before-and-after philosophical reversal is available. Adam's move from conventional creative systems into AI-assisted systems is documented, but the library should not invent his earlier attitude toward AI.

## Things Adam Is Skeptical About

- AI theater, demos, and strategy without operational ownership.
- Calling a governed system “autonomous” when meaningful human approval remains.
- Treating AI-generated volume as a substitute for editorial judgment.
- Overclaiming tools, scale, metrics, or production status.
- Generic SEO products that treat AEO as an add-on rather than designing directly for answer engines.
- Automation without permissions, observability, failure handling, and a safe way to stop or reverse it.

## Privacy, Trust, and Safety

Adam's public systems demonstrate scoped access, identity verification, audit trails, payment gating, prohibited-category controls, human approval, verification states, auto-deactivation, and kill switches. The correct claim is that he designs practical governance into applied systems—not that he is a formal security, privacy, or compliance executive.
