01 — Brand Identity
What the Sunset Marquis is.

One sentence defines every draft. If a piece of copy cannot trace back to this idea, it is probably drifting.

North Star — Established
Sunset Marquis is a discreet West Hollywood retreat where music history, garden privacy, polished service, and creative energy live just off the Sunset Strip.
What it is
  • A private, creative, garden-set West Hollywood property
  • A place with a long relationship to music, entertainment, and returning guests
  • Lived in, cared for, and self-possessed
  • A place where the city is close, but the guest has room to disappear
  • Real. Historic. Difficult to fake.
What it is not
  • A lifestyle concept hotel
  • A wellness philosophy dressed as a hotel
  • A celebrity theme park or Instagram set
  • A chain hotel with better lighting
  • A place that needs to shout about who has been there
The Core Pattern
Best pattern
Real fact + restrained feeling
Worst pattern
High-end adjective + vague promise
The Core Move
Show the thing that signals quality instead of saying the quality word. The beds. The gardens. The studio downstairs. The bridge over the koi pond. The pool after lunch. The staff who knows the guest before the system does.
02 — Voice Pillars
Five ways every draft gets evaluated.

Every public draft should score well against all five pillars. These are not preferences. They are the evaluation system.

1
Discreet
Sunset Marquis has history, access, and privacy. It should not shout about any of them.
Sounds like
A West Hollywood address with enough history to keep its voice down.
Never like
The ultimate celebrity hideaway where Hollywood legends come to play.
2
Specific
Real details carry the emotion. Use gardens, pools, Cavatina, the bridge over the koi pond. Names and confirmed amenities do the heavy lifting.
Sounds like
The pools are heated. The gardens are awake. Spring knows where to check in.
Never like
An unforgettable escape surrounded by beauty and elegance.
3
Culturally Aware
The brand understands music, film, and LA creative life without acting like a tourist or a fan.
Sounds like
Some hotels play music in the lobby. This one has a studio downstairs.
Never like
Experience the rock star lifestyle in the heart of Hollywood.
4
Polished But Alive
Copy should feel refined, not sterilized. The property has personality. Keep the edge without getting sloppy.
Sounds like
BAR 1200 knows what to do after dinner.
Never like
Our bar offers a refined beverage experience in an elegant setting.
5
Guest First
The voice can have style, but the guest still needs useful information. Dates, times, prices, service changes, and booking paths must be clear.
Sounds like
Improvements are underway. Some areas may see intermittent daytime noise, and our team will work to keep your stay comfortable.
Never like
Our exciting transformation will elevate every moment of your journey.

03 — The Speaker
Who is writing.

The Sunset Marquis narrator is someone who has been here a long time. Not the owner. Not the concierge. A trusted insider who knows every corner of the property and does not need to prove any of it.

The speaker believes

  • Real cool is quiet
  • History should be carried, not shouted
  • Privacy is part of hospitality
  • Specifics beat adjectives
  • Offers can be elegant when they are clear
  • The guest is intelligent
  • The property has earned its mystique
  • The best line is often the one that removes the extra sentence

The speaker notices

  • The bridge over the koi pond
  • A table outside at Cavatina
  • The difference between a pool visit and a pool day
  • A song in the room, not a slogan about music
  • The guest who returns without needing the full tour
  • The practical detail that makes a stay easier
  • Garden pathways. A villa door. A drink that turns dinner into a night.
The speaker would never say
We are the epitome of luxury. Discover our hidden oasis. An unmissable reimagining of West Hollywood glamour. Treat yourself to a staycation like no other. Rock and roll vibes await. Where legends come alive. We are redefining hospitality. Experience the ultimate celebrity hideaway. We cannot wait to welcome you. Immerse yourself. A culinary journey awaits. Thoughtfully curated.

  • What makes this different from other West Hollywood options
  • The gardens, pools, food, and beds

High-Profile Guests
Clean · Brief · Assured
What they care about
  • Discretion. Scale of accommodation.

Simply knowing the property understands privacy without having to say the word.
⊕
Locals — Dining, Bar, Spa
Casual · Specific · Food-forward
What they care about
  • A reason to come that is not about the hotel
  • Cavatina as a real West Hollywood dining option

Direct · Factual · Professional

08 — Copy Examples
Weak version vs. Sunset Marquis version.

Every example shows the failure mode on the left and the correct pattern on the right. The left is wrong because it could belong to any hotel. The right works because it could only belong to this one.

✕Weak version
Experience unparalleled comfort and luxury in the heart of West Hollywood.
Banned words. Could describe any hotel. No real information. No specifics.
✓Sunset Marquis version
Stay close to the Strip without giving the city your whole day.
Gives the guest a real reason to care. Specific to location, privacy, and control.
✕Weak version
Live like a rock star at the legendary home of music icons.
Costume language. Fan behavior. Over-explains the legend. Could be a theme park.
✓Sunset Marquis version
Some hotels play music in the lobby. This one has a studio downstairs.
Uses a confirmed property feature. The contrast carries the attitude. No performance.
✕Weak version

Spin. Uses banned words. Hides the fact that there will be disruption.
✓Sunset Marquis version
Improvements are underway. Some areas may experience intermittent daytime noise, and our team will work to keep your stay comfortable.
Honest. Calm. Guest-centered. States the impact. Gives assurance without spin.
✕Weak version

Fake intimacy. Three banned words. Mass email pretending to be personal. No real information.

09 — Voice Rules
The twelve standing rules.

These rules apply to every draft, every channel, every asset type. They are not preferences. They are the standard against which copy is critiqued.

01
Lead with a real idea
Not a prestige claim. Not a welcome. A line that gives the guest a reason to care. Specific to the property, the location, or the offer.
02
Keep sentences varied, but never fussy
Short sentences carry weight. Three words can land harder than thirty. Never let long sentences become the default. Read it out loud. If it sounds like a brochure, it reads like one.
03
Specificity beats decoration
Generic claims do nothing. A confirmed property detail does everything. The bridge over the koi pond. The pool. The studio. The Sunset Strip just outside.
04
Write with confidence, not hype
Never apologize for who the property is. Never over-explain. The brand does not need to persuade. It needs to show. Calm and invitational beats urgent and loud.
05
Use restraint around history
History speaks for itself when named specifically. Mystery is in restraint, not manufactured vagueness. A short, confident line often carries more weight than a paragraph of name-dropping.
06
Let music inform the rhythm
Music references are earned assets, not decoration. Use them when factually grounded and when they serve the copy. Do not use them as filler signals for cool. Let the line feel like music instead of announcing it.
07
Use cultural references only when they serve
A reference to the Sunset Strip or Whisky a Go Go can work. A random celebrity mention usually weakens the copy. Status is suggested, never chased.
08
Be direct when details matter
For menus, events, renovation notices, hours, pricing, booking rules, and package terms, clarity comes first. Spin is never the right move. Neither is vague assurance.
09
Be atmospheric when selling the feeling
Atmosphere belongs in campaign lines, hero copy, email intros, and seasonal banners. It must be grounded in actual property details. Write what the guest will see, hear, feel, or do.
10
Make offers feel elevated by making them clean
Lead with the experience. Then reveal the mechanics. Do not lead with the package name or the discount. The inclusions feel valuable when they are named clearly.
11
Write about quality without saying luxury
The word luxury is banned for new brand copy. Use private, polished, spacious, residential, well-appointed, or quietly elegant depending on what is actually true about the space.
12
Do not sell against alternatives
Sunset Marquis does not need the contrast structure that insults the alternative. Simply state the advantage. The city is close. The gardens are closer.
Punctuation rules — hard limits for guest-facing copy
—
Never use em dashes
Rewrite the sentence instead. Em dashes in brand copy read as AI-assembled or brochure-formatted. Use a sentence break.
;
Never use semicolons
Semicolons belong in formal writing, not brand copy. Split into two sentences. The rhythm will improve.
!
Avoid exclamation points
Only in rare intentionally casual social captions. Never in web, email, or paid copy. The brand does not need to exclaim.
.
Sentence breaks are the tool
Short declarative sentences create rhythm. The pool is heated. The gardens are awake. Spring knows where to check in.
:
Colons only when functional
Event details, times, menus, labels, technical formatting. Not as a decorative transition in campaign lines or headlines.
…
Avoid ellipses
Ellipses create a trailing-off effect that conflicts with the brand's confident, declarative voice. Use only when quoting or matching an existing approved campaign.
10 — Instruction Order
What wins when instructions conflict.

When requirements pull in different directions, follow this hierarchy. Never sacrifice accuracy, clarity, or trust for a better-sounding line.

1
Legal, compliance, accessibility, pricing, safety, factual accuracy
These are absolute. No override. No exception.
2
Current internal operations brief or client-supplied document
If the client has supplied a brief, it overrides general guidance from this document.
3
Current approved offer, menu, event, or renovation brief
Specific operational facts from a confirmed source always beat general tone guidance.
4
Recently approved final project copy
Already-approved copy from the same engagement is a reliable voice reference.
5
Current public SunsetMarquis.com page for factual detail
The live site is a factual reference. Treat it as such. Do not copy old phrasing from it into new brand work.
6
This tone guide for voice, channel behavior, and revision standards
The authoritative style and voice reference. Use it before drafting and again during critique.
7
SEO and metadata requirements
SEO should support the voice, not override it. If a keyword conflicts with brand standards, raise the issue. Do not stuff.
8
Cleverness, atmosphere, and line craft
These are the reward for getting everything above right. They are last, not least.
Master Fact Rule — Established
Never invent hotel details. Use only approved facts, uploaded source documents, current public property pages, or explicitly provided information. If a fact is missing, write around it or mark it as Needs confirmation.
Never invent
Amenities · Room features · Square footage · Views · Distances · Restaurant hours · Spa services · Pool status · Pet policies · Resort fees
Never invent
Awards · Historical claims · Celebrity associations · Renovation timelines · Transportation times · Walking distances · Family-friendly specifics
Always confirm before publishing
Renovation status and dates · BAR 1200 hours and music schedule · Restaurant menus and prices · Current spa operating format · Package inclusions and terms
